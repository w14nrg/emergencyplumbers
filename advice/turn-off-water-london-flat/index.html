How to turn off the water in a London flat

In a London flat, the stopcock is rarely where you'd expect. Finding it now — before anything goes wrong — is the single most useful thing you can do.

Where to look

  1. Under the kitchen sink. The most common place, usually on the pipe that feeds the cold tap.
  2. Where the supply comes in. Look for a pipe coming up through the floor — in a hallway, under the stairs, or in a cupboard near the front door.
  3. In an airing cupboard near the hot water cylinder, if you have one.
  4. Behind a panel — a bath panel, a kickboard under kitchen units or a boxed-in pipe run.
  5. Outside the flat. In converted houses, an upper flat's stopcock can be in the communal hallway, under the stairs or in the basement flat.

Turning it off

Turn the handle clockwise until it stops — "righty-tighty". Don't force it with tools if it won't move; old stopcocks can snap. Then open a cold tap to check the water has stopped. Some water will drain out of the pipes for a minute or two.

Newer installations may have a lever valve instead: turn the lever so it's across the pipe, not along it.

Mansion blocks and converted houses

Many London blocks are supplied from communal tanks or a pumped riser. Your flat may have its own stopcock, but some flats — especially in older blocks — rely on a valve in a riser cupboard, on the landing or in the basement. Ask your porter, managing agent or freeholder now where the valve for your flat is and who holds the key.

In converted houses, check that your stopcock actually controls only your flat. Some conversions have one stopcock for the whole building.

The outside stop tap

There's usually a stop tap under a small cover in the pavement outside, which belongs to the water company. It needs a special key and can be stiff or buried. It's useful in an emergency, but don't force it.

Test it before you need it

Once or twice a year, turn your stopcock off and on again. If it's stiff, seized or leaks when you turn it, get it replaced before an emergency — see stopcock repair.

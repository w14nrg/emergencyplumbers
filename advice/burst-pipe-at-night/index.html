Burst pipe at night: the first 10 minutes

What you do in the first few minutes decides how much damage a burst pipe causes. Here's the order to do things in.

Minute 0–2: stop the water

Find the main stopcock and turn it clockwise until it stops. In flats it's often under the kitchen sink, in a hallway or airing cupboard, or where the supply pipe comes up through the floor. If you can't find it or it won't turn, look for a small isolating valve on the pipe nearest the leak — a slot you turn a quarter-turn with a screwdriver.

If your water comes from a tank in the loft (common in older houses with a hot water cylinder), closing the valve on the pipe coming out of the tank also stops the flow.

Minute 2–4: stay safe

If water is near sockets, light fittings or the consumer unit, don't touch them. Only switch off the electricity at the consumer unit if you can reach it without standing in water. If a ceiling light is dripping, stay out of that room.

Minute 4–6: drain the system

Open all the cold taps and flush each toilet once. That empties the pipes so less water escapes. If the burst is on a hot pipe or the heating, turn off the boiler and the immersion heater too.

Minute 6–8: limit the damage

  • Put buckets, bowls and towels down; move rugs, electronics and furniture.
  • If you're in a flat, knock on the door below or call them — they may be getting water through their ceiling.
  • If the ceiling is bulging, keep people out from underneath it.

Minute 8–10: get help

Take photos and a short video of the leak, the damage and the stopcock, then send them to us with your postcode. Tell us whether water is still coming out after you've turned the stopcock. Those photos also help with an insurance claim.

If the water won't stop

If water is still pouring after you've closed the stopcock, the stopcock may not be working, the pipe may be fed from a tank, or it may be on a shared supply in a block. Call us rather than messaging. In a block, contact the porter or managing agent's emergency line too.

Need someone now?

Send your postcode and a photo, or call. We'll tell you honestly how soon we can get there and what it will cost.

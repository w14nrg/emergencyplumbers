Shower leaks

Shower leaking into the room below

A shower leak usually shows up on the ceiling underneath long before you see it in the bathroom. The key question is whether it leaks only when the shower is running, or all the time.

Do this now

  1. Stop using the shower.
  2. Watch the ceiling below: does the drip stop when the shower is off?
  3. If it keeps leaking with the shower off, turn off the stopcock — the leak is on a supply pipe.
  4. If it's a flat below you, let them know straight away.
  5. Photograph the ceiling below and the shower tray, seals and grout.

Only when the shower is running, or all the time?

Only when it's used

Usually a failed silicone seal or grout, a leaking waste or trap under the tray, a cracked tray, or water escaping past the shower screen or door seals.

All the time

A leak on the hot or cold supply to the shower valve, or on the valve itself — often hidden behind tiles. This one needs isolating straight away.

How we find the leak

We test each part separately: running water only into the waste, only onto the tray, then onto the walls and screen, while watching for water below. That tells us whether it's the waste, the tray, the seals or the enclosure — without pulling everything apart.

  • Waste and trap leaks can often be reached from below the tray or through an access panel, and repaired on the visit.
  • Failed seals can be cut out and re-sealed, but the new seal needs time to cure before the shower is used.
  • Concealed valve or pipe leaks are isolated on the night. A permanent repair may mean removing tiles, which we'll explain and quote separately.
  • Cracked trays need replacing; we'll isolate the shower and explain the options.

Electric showers

If the leak is from an electric shower unit, switch it off at its pull-cord or isolator and don't touch the unit while it's wet. We can deal with the water side; the electrical side may need an electrician.

Questions

Can I keep using the shower until it's fixed?

If it leaks only when used, using it will keep soaking the ceiling below. Use another bathroom or the bath if you can until it's repaired.

Is it covered by insurance?

Escape of water damage often is, but failed seals and grout are usually treated as wear and tear. A written note of what failed helps with any claim.

Burst pipes

Burst pipe repair

Water spraying from a pipe, pouring from a joint or running down a wall needs stopping now. Turn the water off first, then send us a photo or call.

Do this now

  1. Turn off the water at the main stopcock (clockwise). If it won't move, see stopcock won't turn off.
  2. Open all the cold taps to drain the pipes, and flush the toilets once.
  3. If the burst is on heating pipework, turn the boiler off. If you have a hot water cylinder, switch off the immersion heater.
  4. Keep away from wet sockets, switches and light fittings.
  5. Move valuables, put down towels and buckets, and warn the flat below.
  6. Photograph the damage for your insurer, then contact us.
If water is near sockets, light fittings or the consumer unit, don't touch them. Only switch the electricity off at the consumer unit if you can reach it without standing in water or touching anything wet. If you can't, keep everyone away from that area and get an electrician. If the power supply itself looks damaged, the free 105 line reaches your local electricity network.

Is it really a burst?

"Burst pipe" covers several different faults, and the fix depends on which one you have:

Split pipe

A crack along copper or plastic pipe, often from frost, a nail or screw through a floor, or old pipe wearing thin. Usually sprays hard.

Failed joint

A compression nut that has worked loose, a soldered joint that has let go, or a push-fit fitting that has slipped. Often a steady pour.

Pinhole leak

A tiny hole in old copper, common under floors and in walls. Can drip unnoticed for weeks before it shows on a ceiling.

Flexible hose

The braided hoses under sinks and on taps, toilets and washing machines. When they fail, they fail suddenly and at full mains pressure.

What we do when we arrive

  1. Make sure the water is off. If the main stopcock isn't holding, we isolate further back — at the outside stop tap, the communal valve in a mansion block, or on the tank feed.
  2. Find exactly where it's leaking. Often obvious; sometimes it means lifting a floorboard or removing a bath panel. We open up as little as possible.
  3. Repair it. We cut out the damaged section and replace it with new pipe and fittings, or replace the failed joint or hose.
  4. Turn the water back on and test. We restore the supply slowly, check every joint we've touched and check the rest of the run for weeping.

What can be fixed on the night

Most bursts on standard 15mm and 22mm copper or plastic pipe, failed fittings and burst hoses can be repaired on the visit. Things that may need a return visit:

  • Pipes buried in solid floors or chased into plastered walls, where reaching them properly means more opening up.
  • Lead or very old iron supply pipe, where a lasting repair may mean replacing a length of the supply.
  • Damage that affects a hot water cylinder or tank.

In those cases we make it safe and, where we can, restore water to the rest of the property while the affected section stays isolated.

Bursts in London flats

In converted houses and mansion blocks, the stopcock for your flat may not stop the burst at all — especially if the pipe is a shared rising main or the feed to a communal tank. If turning your stopcock doesn't slow the water, tell us straight away. We may need the porter, managing agent or a neighbour to reach the right valve.

Frozen pipes

A frozen pipe usually bursts when it thaws, not when it freezes. If a pipe is frozen, turn off the water before it thaws and warm it gently with a hairdryer or warm cloths, working from the tap end back. Never use a blowtorch or naked flame.

Insurance

Most home insurance covers "escape of water" damage, but usually not the cost of the repair to the pipe itself. Photograph the damage before you clear up, keep wet items if you can, and ask us for a written note of what failed and what we did.

Questions

Can I use a pipe repair clamp or tape?

An emergency clamp or self-amalgamating tape can slow a small leak on a straight run of pipe until we arrive. It won't hold on a joint or on high-pressure sprays, and it isn't a permanent repair.

Should I turn the boiler off?

If the leak is on heating pipework or near the boiler, yes. If it's a cold supply pipe, turning off the main stopcock is the priority. With the water off, a combi boiler can't heat water anyway.

Why does water keep coming after I've turned off the stopcock?

Some water drains out of the pipes for a few minutes. If it keeps flowing, the stopcock may not be closing fully, the leak may be fed from a tank in the loft, or it may be on a shared supply. Tell us which and we'll advise.

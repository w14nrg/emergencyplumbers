Converted terraces and mansion flats

Much of W14 is Victorian terraced housing split into two, three or four flats, alongside red-brick mansion blocks such as those around Queen's Club Gardens and a number of large post-war estates. Each comes with its own plumbing quirks:

  • Converted houses: the flats often share one rising main, and the stopcock for an upper flat can be in the hallway, under the stairs or in the basement flat. Waste pipes from upper bathrooms frequently run through the flats below.
  • Mansion blocks: supplies typically come from communal tanks or a pumped riser, with isolation valves in a riser cupboard rather than in each flat.
  • Estates: purpose-built blocks with communal heating or shared stacks, where some valves are controlled by the estate's maintenance team.

Emergencies we see in West Kensington

The callouts we expect most here are water coming through a ceiling from the flat above, leaks from old waste pipes that pass through other flats, stopcocks that haven't been turned in decades and seize when they're needed, and blocked toilets on shared soil stacks — where the lower flat floods first.

In converted houses, it's worth knowing before an emergency where your stopcock is and whether it actually controls only your flat. See our guide to turning off the water in a London flat.

Getting to you

Tell us when you message if we need to get into a communal riser, a basement or a neighbouring flat. For mansion blocks, the porter or the managing agent's out-of-hours number can make the difference between isolating a leak in minutes or an hour. Parking in W14 is mostly controlled by residents' permit zones; we'll sort out where to park.

Coverage

W14 is in our core area, which means it's one of the areas we can reach most reliably out of hours. When you contact us we'll give you a realistic arrival time for that night — we don't quote fixed response times on the website because they depend on traffic, time and what else is going on.

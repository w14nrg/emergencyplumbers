Stopcocks and water supply

Stopcock won't turn off — or there's no water

The stopcock is the one valve you need to work in an emergency. If it's seized, just spins or is leaking itself, you need it sorted before anything else can be fixed.

Do this now

  1. Try turning the stopcock clockwise firmly by hand. Don't use a long lever or hammer on it — old brass can snap.
  2. If it won't close, look for other valves: an isolating valve on the pipe feeding the leaking fitting, or a valve on the tank feed in the loft.
  3. In a block of flats, ask the porter or managing agent about a valve on the riser or communal supply.
  4. If you know where the outside stop tap is and have the key, it can stop the supply from the pavement.
  5. Open the cold taps to drain the pipes while you wait.

What's wrong with the stopcock?

Seized

The handle won't move. Common on stopcocks that haven't been turned in years, especially in hard-water London. Forcing it risks breaking the spindle.

Turns but doesn't stop the water

The handle turns but water keeps flowing. The washer (jumper) inside has worn or come loose, or the spindle has snapped internally.

Leaking from the stopcock

Water around the spindle (a gland leak) or from the joints either side. Often starts after the stopcock is finally turned.

Can't find it

Boxed in, under kitchen units, behind a bath panel or in a communal cupboard. It's worth finding now, before you need it.

How we fix it

  1. Isolate the supply further back. At the outside stop tap, a communal valve, or with a pipe freezing kit on the supply pipe.
  2. Repair or replace the stopcock. Re-packing the gland can stop a spindle leak; otherwise we fit a new stopcock or a quarter-turn valve that's easier to close in future.
  3. Restore and test. We turn the supply back on slowly and check it closes fully.

Many older London properties still have lead supply pipes. If yours does, we'll tell you — a lead pipe affects what kind of fitting can be used and whether a longer-term replacement is worth planning.

No water at all?

  1. Check whether the neighbours have water. If they don't, it's almost certainly a supply problem — check your water company's website or service updates. In most of London that's Thames Water.
  2. Check your stopcock hasn't been turned off, and that any isolating valves are open.
  3. If only the cold kitchen tap works but nothing else, the cold water tank in the loft may be empty — often a stuck float valve.
  4. In winter, a frozen pipe can cut the supply. Turn off the stopcock before it thaws in case it has split.

If neighbours have water and you don't, message us — the cause is inside your property and we can find it.

Questions

Am I allowed to use the outside stop tap?

The outside stop tap is part of the water company's network, but householders can use it in an emergency if they have the right key and can reach it. If you're not sure or it's stiff, don't force it — tell us and we'll deal with it.

How often should I turn my stopcock?

Turning it off and on again once or twice a year helps stop it seizing. If it's stiff, don't force it — have it replaced before an emergency.

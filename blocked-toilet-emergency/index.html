Blocked toilets

Blocked or overflowing toilet

If it's your only toilet, it's an emergency. The question is whether the blockage is in the toilet itself, or further down in the soil stack or drain.

Do this now

  1. Stop flushing. Each flush adds water that has nowhere to go.
  2. If the bowl is filling, take the cistern lid off and push the float up, or turn off the toilet's isolating valve (a small screw on the supply pipe).
  3. Check other fittings: does the bath, shower or ground-floor gully gurgle or back up when the toilet is used?
  4. Try a flanged toilet plunger with slow, firm strokes. Don't use chemical drain cleaners in a blocked toilet.
  5. Put on gloves and lay down old towels if water has spilled.

Toilet blockage or drain blockage?

Blockage in the toilet

Only the toilet is affected. The bowl fills when you flush and drains slowly afterwards. Other basins, baths and showers drain normally. Usually wipes, sanitary items, too much paper or an object stuck in the trap.

Blockage further down

The bath, shower or basin gurgles or fills when the toilet is flushed, or a ground-floor gully or manhole is overflowing. The problem is in the soil stack or underground drain, not the toilet.

In mansion blocks and converted houses, several flats often share one soil stack. If a stack is blocked, the lowest flat is usually the one that floods — sometimes with other people's waste. If you're on a lower floor and the toilet is rising when you haven't flushed, tell us immediately and ask neighbours above to stop using water.

What we do when we arrive

  1. Check where the blockage is. We test other fittings and, where it's accessible, look at the stack and the nearest inspection chamber.
  2. Clear it. Most toilet blockages clear with a professional plunger or a toilet auger, which reaches through the trap without scratching the pan.
  3. Remove the pan if needed. If something solid is wedged in the outlet, we may need to take the toilet off to remove it, then refit it with a new seal.
  4. Test and clean up. We flush several times to check it's clear, and clean and disinfect the area we've worked in.

If the problem is the drain

If the blockage is in the underground drain rather than inside your property, it may need drain rods or jetting from a manhole. We'll tell you what we find. Since October 2011, drains shared with neighbours and lateral drains outside your property boundary have generally been the water company's responsibility in England — in most of London that's Thames Water — so it may be worth reporting to them before paying for drain work.

What not to do

  • Don't keep flushing to "push it through".
  • Don't pour chemical drain cleaner into a blocked WC — it sits in the bowl and can splash back when the toilet is worked on.
  • Don't use a sink plunger without a flange; it won't seal on a toilet outlet.
  • Don't use a wire coat hanger in the trap — it scratches the glaze.

Questions

Is a blocked toilet really an emergency?

If it's your only toilet, or it's overflowing, yes. If you have another working toilet, it can usually wait for a daytime visit.

Why does my toilet keep blocking?

Repeated blockages usually mean wet wipes or sanitary items, a partial blockage further down, a poorly installed pan connector, or a soil pipe with too little fall. We'll look for the cause, not just clear the symptom.

Do you clear macerator toilets?

We can look at blocked or noisy macerators, but if the unit has failed it may need a replacement unit, which we'll quote for separately.

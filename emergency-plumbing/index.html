Emergency plumbing

Emergency plumbing services

If water is escaping and you can't stop it, that's an emergency. So is a blocked only toilet, a stopcock that won't close, or a leak into the flat below. Here's what to do and how we deal with each one.

Do this now

  1. Turn off the water at the stopcock. In most flats it's under the kitchen sink, in a hallway cupboard or where the pipe enters the property.
  2. Open the cold taps to drain water out of the pipes.
  3. If the leak is from heating pipes or a radiator, turn the boiler off.
  4. Keep away from wet sockets and light fittings.
  5. Take photos and a short video, then message or call us.

Is it an emergency?

A plumbing problem is an emergency when waiting will cause damage, leave you without basic facilities, or put someone at risk. As a rule of thumb:

Call tonight

  • Water escaping that you can't stop
  • Water coming through a ceiling or into another flat
  • Your only toilet is blocked or overflowing
  • The stopcock won't close or is itself leaking
  • No water at all, and the neighbours have water
  • A leaking cylinder or tank

Usually fine until morning

  • A dripping tap you can turn off at its isolating valve
  • A slow-draining basin when other basins work
  • A small weep you can catch in a bowl and isolate
  • A second toilet that's blocked when another works
  • Low pressure that hasn't changed

If you're not sure, send us a photo. We'll tell you honestly whether it needs someone tonight or can wait for a cheaper daytime visit.

What we don't do

We don't do gas work of any kind, including boiler repairs; that needs a Gas Safe registered engineer. If you can smell gas, leave the property and call 0800 111 999. If a blockage turns out to be in the underground drain and needs jetting or a camera survey, we'll tell you and point you to the right people.

What it costs

Charges are set in time bands — weekday daytime, evenings and weekends, overnight, and bank holidays — with a first-hour charge and then half-hourly time. We tell you the charge for your time of day before anyone sets off, and any parts are agreed before they're fitted. How our pricing works.

London properties make leaks harder

Much of West and Central London is converted period houses and mansion blocks. That means shared supply pipes, stopcocks in odd places, communal tanks in the roof and soil stacks that serve several flats. A leak in one flat often shows up in another. We're used to tracing water through these buildings and working with porters and managing agents to reach risers and plant rooms at night.

Leaking radiators

Leaking radiator and radiator valve repair

Most radiator leaks come from the valve, not the radiator. Where exactly it's leaking tells you whether it's a quick fix or a replacement.

Do this now

  1. Turn the heating off at the boiler or controller and let the system cool.
  2. Close both radiator valves: turn the thermostatic or handwheel valve to off, then turn the lockshield (the capped valve) clockwise with a spanner, counting the turns so it can be reset.
  3. Put a towel and a container under the leak.
  4. Check the boiler pressure gauge. If it has dropped below about 1 bar, the system has lost water — don't top it up repeatedly while it's still leaking.
  5. Photograph exactly where the water is coming from and send it to us.

Where is it leaking?

Valve gland (spindle)

Water around the spindle under the head or cap. Common on older valves. Often fixable by re-packing or tightening the gland, or replacing the valve head.

Union nut

Water from the nut joining the valve to the radiator tail. The joint may need re-making with new sealing, or a new tail.

Valve body

Water weeping from a crack or corroded area on the valve itself. The valve needs replacing.

Pipework or radiator

A pinhole in the pipe or the radiator panel, usually from internal corrosion. Pipe can be repaired; a corroded radiator normally needs replacing.

A leaking bleed valve at the top corner of a radiator usually just needs closing or a new bleed screw.

How we repair it

  1. Isolate the radiator. If the valves won't close properly — common with old valves — we may need to drain part of the system or use a pipe freezing kit.
  2. Repair or replace. We re-pack glands, re-make union joints and replace valves or sections of pipe. A pipe freezing kit lets us change a valve without draining the whole system in many cases.
  3. Refill and repressurise. On sealed systems we refill to the correct pressure and bleed the air out.
  4. Check the rest. We check the boiler restarts and the other radiators heat up.

We work on the water side of heating systems only. If the boiler itself needs attention, you'll need a Gas Safe registered engineer.

What can wait

A slow weep you can catch in a container, with the radiator isolated, can usually wait for a daytime visit. Call us at night if the leak won't stop, water is reaching a ceiling below, or the system keeps losing pressure.

Questions

Can I just tighten the nut?

A small nip on a weeping gland nut can stop a minor leak. Overtightening a union nut can crack an old valve or distort the joint, so if it doesn't stop with a gentle quarter-turn, leave it.

Why does my boiler pressure keep dropping?

On a sealed heating system, a pressure drop means water is escaping somewhere — a radiator valve, a joint, a pinhole under the floor, or the boiler's pressure relief valve discharging outside. We'll look for the leak on the water side.
